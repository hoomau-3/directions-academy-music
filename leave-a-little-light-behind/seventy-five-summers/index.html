What this song holds

Leave a Little Light Behind · Song 1 of 3

A grandfather at seventy-five looks backward and forward at the same time. He is grateful for porch lights, handshakes, and neighbors who walked right in. His worry is not that the world changed. It is his granddaughter walking with her head down, ten thousand friends online and not a single word. So he tells his story slowly, hoping someone young hears the love inside it.

To think about

  • Who first made you feel that you mattered?
  • What did your grandparents' world have that yours could use?
  • Who could you look in the eye this week and tell they matter?

Lyrics

I was born when the porch light meant “come on home,”
When a handshake was a signature, and you were never alone.
Screen door slammin’, supper on the stove,
Neighbors just walked in, nobody had to knock.
We didn’t have much, but we had each other
A dollar and a dime, and I’d have shared it with my brother.

Lord, I’ve seen the world go from black and white to blinding light,
And I wouldn’t trade a single one of my seventy-five years.

I’m grateful for the time I got to live in,
The slow-down Sundays, the front porch grins.
I miss it, sure, but that ain’t the worst of my fears
It’s watching my grandkids grow up cold in a world full of tears.
I just want somebody to look ’em in the eye,
Tell ’em they matter before they say goodbye.

Now everybody’s talkin’ but nobody’s heard,
Ten thousand friends online and not a single word.
I watch my granddaughter walkin’ with her head down low,
Starin’ at a screen full of people she don’t know.
I’m not sayin’ that it’s all gone bad,
But I miss the kindness that we used to have.

Lord, I’ve seen the world speed up till it left the heart behind,
And all I want’s for love to catch up in time.

(Chorus repeats)

So I’ll sit right here and tell my story,
Not for the past, but for what’s still comin’.
Maybe if I say it slow enough,
Somebody young will hear the love
In how we used to live
And give it back again.

I’m grateful for the time I got to live in,
Every hard-won day, every front porch grin.
And if I leave one thing before I close my eyes,
Let it be some warmth for my grandkids’ lives.
So look each other in the eye,
Tell somebody that they matter
Before you say goodbye.
