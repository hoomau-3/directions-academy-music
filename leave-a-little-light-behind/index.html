Leave a Little Light Behind

A collection of songs written from the season of life where memory becomes gratitude, concern becomes hope, and love becomes legacy.

Three songs. Three reflections. One conversation between generations.

These songs grew from a realization that arrived somewhere around my seventy-fifth year: the older I get, the more I find myself looking backward and forward at the same time.

Backward toward the people who taught me what matters. Forward toward the people who will inherit whatever light we leave behind. In between is a simple question: what is still worth passing on?

A gentle note

These songs come from real lives, and some of them are tender. They touch on growing older, the loss of a wife, and worry for the generation coming up.

About this album

Looking back, I see a life filled with family gatherings, Sunday suppers, laughter around crowded tables, lifelong friendships, hard-earned lessons, and acts of kindness that seemed ordinary at the time but became priceless with age. I was fortunate enough to grow up in a world where doors were left unlocked, neighbors became family, and people measured wealth less by what they owned than by who would show up when they needed help.

Looking forward, I see my grandchildren inheriting a very different world. Faster. Louder. More connected than ever, yet often strangely disconnected from the people sitting right beside them. Like many grandparents, I sometimes wonder how to share the lessons that shaped my life without asking them to live in the past.

These songs are my attempt to answer that question.

Seventy-Five Summers stands at the crossroads of gratitude and concern. It celebrates a lifetime of blessings while wrestling with the challenge of reaching a younger generation growing up in a world far different from the one that shaped their grandparents.

Stories That Remain explores the enduring power of love after loss, reminding us that the people who shape our lives never truly leave us. They continue living in the stories we tell, the lives they touched, and the memories that return when we need them most.

Slow-Down Sundays brings the journey home. It recognizes that every generation must find its own path and that perhaps our responsibility is not to preserve the past, but to pass forward its best qualities: kindness, connection, gratitude, laughter, and the simple courage to look another human being in the eye and let them know they matter.

Musically, these songs are rooted in traditional country storytelling, but Slow-Down Sundays reaches even further back, drawing inspiration from the classic country-western ballads of an earlier era. With pedal steel, piano, acoustic guitar, upright bass, and brushed snare, the music echoes a time when songs unfolded slowly and stories were allowed to breathe.

But this album isn't really about yesterday.

It's about remembering what made yesterday meaningful and carrying those lessons into tomorrow.

If there is a message running through all three songs, it is this:

The world is still beautiful.
The people we love still matter.
Kindness still matters.
Family still matters.
The stars still shine.
Stories still matter.

And no matter how much the world changes, nothing will ever replace a shared meal, a heartfelt conversation, a hand to hold, or the simple gift of being fully present with one another.

These songs are not instructions from one generation to another.

They are love letters.
To those who came before us.
To those who walk beside us.
And most of all, to those who will come after us.

So slow down.
Look around.
Tell somebody they matter.
And don't wait too long to visit home.

A grandfather standing between memory and hope, looking backward with gratitude, forward with love, and leaving a little light behind for those who follow.
