Stages Not Ages

Stages Not Ages follows the journey of a lifetime through nine songs, from a baby's first lesson in trust to the questions and wisdom of very old age.

Inspired by the work of Erik Erikson, each song explores a challenge we all face: learning independence, finding identity, building relationships, caring for others, making peace with our past, and remaining ourselves through every season of life.

The title says it all: stages, not ages. We revisit old questions throughout our lives, meeting them in new ways. Wherever you are on the journey, there is a place for you here.

A gentle note

These songs come from real lives and honest experiences. They touch on childhood wounds, loneliness, love, regret, aging, loss, resilience, and the search for meaning.

Listen at your own pace. Pause when you need to.

How to listen

There is no right way to experience Stages Not Ages. You can begin at Stage 1 and follow the journey from infancy to old age, or start with the stage that speaks most directly to your life today.

Parents may find it meaningful to listen to their child's stage alongside their own. Each song offers a glimpse into a season of life. Every stage includes its own page with the video and lyrics.

The songs

  1. Trust These ArmsApprox. birth to 18-24 months
    Stage 1: Trust vs. Mistrust. Can I trust the world to hold me?
  2. Safe to Make a Little MessApprox. 18-24 months to 3 years
    Stage 2: Autonomy vs. Shame and Doubt. Can I stand on my own two feet?
  3. Allowed to WantApprox. 3 to 5 years
    Stage 3: Initiative vs. Guilt. Do I dare turn my ideas into action?
  4. Build It CrookedApprox. 6 to 11 years
    Stage 4: Industry vs. Inferiority. Do my efforts matter?
  5. Room to BecomeApprox. 12 to 18-20 years
    Stage 5: Identity vs. Role Confusion. Who am I becoming?
  6. Without Losing MyselfApprox. 18 to 40 years
    Stage 6: Intimacy vs. Isolation. Can I be close without losing myself?
  7. Learning to CareApprox. 40 to 65 years
    Stage 7: Generativity vs. Stagnation. Who am I doing all of this for?
  8. Okay to Have Been MeApprox. 65 to 80 years
    Stage 8: Integrity vs. Despair. Can I make peace with the life I've lived?
  9. Tell the Family I Am OkayApprox. 80 and up
    Stage 9: Very Old Age. One final gift: wisdom shared with those who remain.
