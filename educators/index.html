For Teachers, Nurses, and Counselors

Free songs and listening guides for the people who teach human development, prepare new parents, and sit with families.

Every song on this site carries one small life lesson, built on Erik Erikson's psychosocial stages: the same framework taught in human development, nursing, and early childhood courses. Each song has its own page with the video and the full lyrics, free, with no ads and no sign-up.

How to use a song in class or clinic

  1. Play the song. Each runs three to seven minutes. The video and lyrics are on the song's page.
  2. Read the lyrics together. Aloud or silently. Printing them from the song page works well.
  3. Open with one question. The guides below give you three per song. One is usually enough.

Guide 1: Stages Not Ages

Nine songs, one per stage, for human development and nursing courses, or for anyone meeting Erikson for the first time. The album page is here.

StageThe question and the songDiscussion questions
Stage 1
Trust vs. Mistrust
Birth to 18-24 months		Is the world safe?
Trust These Arms
  • When a baby cries, what are they learning about asking for help?
  • Who taught you whether people can be counted on?
  • What helps a tired caregiver stay steady when nothing soothes?
Stage 2
Autonomy vs. Shame and Doubt
18-24 months to 3 years		Can I do it myself?
Safe to Make a Little Mess
  • Why is a toddler's “no” a healthy sign?
  • Where is the line between keeping a child safe and keeping a child stopped?
  • How was your own “me do it” answered when you were small?
Stage 3
Initiative vs. Guilt
3 to 5 years		I have ideas. Can I try things?
Allowed to Want
  • How do adults answer ideas that arrive at inconvenient moments?
  • What happens to wanting when every idea is swatted down?
  • What did you learn, growing up, about asking for what you wanted?
Stage 4
Industry vs. Inferiority
6 to 12 years		Am I good at anything?
Build It Crooked
  • What is the difference between praising the finish and praising the effort?
  • Where are children in your care allowed to be beginners?
  • What happened when you brought home something imperfect?
Stage 5
Identity vs. Role Confusion
Adolescence		Who am I?
Room to Become
  • Why is experimenting the actual work of adolescence?
  • Can a teen differ from their family and still be close to it?
  • Who gave you room to become, and who did not?
Stage 6
Intimacy vs. Isolation
About 18 to 40		Can I love without losing myself?
Without Losing Myself
  • What does it mean to be known rather than performed?
  • What might the packed suitcase by the door stand for?
  • What would it look like to stay close and stay yourself?
Stage 7
Generativity vs. Stagnation
About 40 to 65		Who am I doing all this for?
Learning to Care
  • Who receives your days?
  • What are you building that will outlast your job title?
  • Where could caring replace accumulating this year?
Stage 8
Integrity vs. Despair
About 65 to 80		Is it okay to have been me?
Okay to Have Been Me
  • What does it mean that integrity is not a perfect record?
  • Which chapters of a life are hardest to make peace with?
  • What do you want the people you love to know while you can still tell them?
Stage 9
Very Old Age (Joan Erikson's added stage)
About 80 and up		Am I still me?
Tell the Family I Am Okay
  • How is needing help different from disappearing?
  • When helping someone very old, do we still ask what they want?
  • What changes if we see slowness as effort, not absence?

Guide 2: Parenting by Design

Four songs for childbirth educators, doulas, home visitors, and parenting classes. Best taken in order. The album page is here.

Autopilot

The way we were raised becomes our default setting, and we replay it until we notice.

  • What is one moment lately when you heard your own parents in your voice?
  • Which parts of your upbringing do you want to pass on, and which do you want to retire?
  • What helps you notice the autopilot before it flies the plane?

No Manual

Nobody is handed a manual with the baby. Overwhelm is normal, and asking for help is strength, not failure.

  • What did you believe about parenting before it began, and what has surprised you?
  • Where could you ask for help this week, and what stops you?
  • What would you tell a brand-new parent on their first night home?

The Pattern Stops With You

Patterns travel through families until one person sees them clearly and chooses differently.

  • What pattern in your family would you like to stop with you?
  • What does it cost to be the one who stops it?
  • Who can stand beside you while you do?

The Stage They're In

Behavior that looks like defiance is often just the stage. Naming the stage turns frustration into patience.

  • Think of a child's most trying behavior. What might it look like through the lens of their stage?
  • How does naming the stage change what an adult does next?
  • What stage are you in, and what does it need?

A note on the Becoming album

Becoming tells one girl's story across twelve songs, for adolescents and the adults who love them. It touches on heavy ground: feeling unseen, body image, heartbreak, and becoming a mother young. Counselors and health teachers may find it powerful, and we ask that you listen through it yourself first. Its album page carries a gentle note with support lines, including 988.
