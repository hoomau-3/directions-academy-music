For Educators, Nurses, and Counselors

Free songs and listening guides for the people who teach human development, support parents, and sit with families.

Every song on this site carries one small life lesson, drawn from Erik Erikson's psychosocial stages, the same framework used in human development, nursing, and early childhood education. Each song has its own page with a free video, full lyrics, and printable discussion guides, with no ads and no sign-up.

How to use a song in class or clinic

  1. Play the song. Most run three to seven minutes. The video and lyrics are on each song's page.
  2. Read the lyrics together, aloud or silently. Printing them from the song page works well.
  3. Start with one question. The guides below offer three for each song, but one is usually enough.

Guide 1: Stages Not Ages

Nine songs, one for each of Erikson's stages, with videos, lyrics, reflection questions, and printable guides. Designed for human development and nursing courses, or for anyone meeting Erikson for the first time. The album page is here.

Guide 2: Parenting by Design

Seven songs for childbirth educators, doulas, home visitors, and parenting classes. Together they follow the journey from pregnancy to raising a child. Best taken in order. The album page is here.

Autopilot

The way we were raised becomes our default setting, and we replay it until we notice.

  • What is one moment lately when you heard your own parents in your voice?
  • Which parts of your upbringing do you want to pass on, and which do you want to retire?
  • What helps you notice the autopilot before it flies the plane?

No Manual

Nobody is handed a manual with the baby. Overwhelm is normal, and asking for help is strength, not failure.

  • What did you believe about parenting before it began, and what has surprised you?
  • Where could you ask for help this week, and what stops you?
  • What would you tell a brand-new parent on their first night home?

The Pattern Stops With You

Patterns travel through families until one person sees them clearly and chooses differently.

  • What pattern in your family would you like to stop with you?
  • What does it cost to be the one who stops it?
  • Who can stand beside you while you do?

The Stage They're In

Behavior that looks like defiance is often just the stage. Naming the stage turns frustration into patience.

  • Think of a child's most trying behavior. What might it look like through the lens of their stage?
  • How does naming the stage change what an adult does next?
  • What stage are you in, and what does it need?

May You Have Enough

A blessing for the road: not an easy life, but enough to carry a child through.

  • What do you wish for the people you love: an easy life, or enough?
  • Which hard season in your life made you stronger, or kinder?
  • What blessing would you like to say out loud to your child?

The Question Shifted Gears

Success stops being the point when a child calls for help. What fills a life is the people we do it for.

  • Who are you really doing it all for?
  • What have you been chasing to make the quiet go away?
  • What is one small way to turn back toward someone you love this week?

The Ten Rules

Life is the classroom. Mistakes are lessons, and growing never ends, for children or for parents.

  • What lesson keeps coming back to you in different clothes?
  • What mistake turned out to be a doorway?
  • What would change if you saw yourself as someone still becoming?

Guide 3: Listen To Your Heart

Three songs, each told twice: once for him and once for her. For health teachers, school counselors, youth groups, and anyone talking with teens about first love. Both are told they are too young to know what love is. Through heartbreak, outside advice and fear, they learn to trust their own heart.

In Erikson's terms, this is Stage 5, identity, meeting the first questions of Stage 6, intimacy. Play each pair side by side: his version, then hers. The songs touch on heartbreak and family pressure, so listen first; the album page, here, has a gentle note and support resources.

The Last Letter to Her and The Last Letter to Him

A month after a breakup, both are still replaying it, looking for what they could have fixed. He turns toward the one who stayed. She chooses herself.

  • Why do we replay a breakup looking for the one moment we could have fixed?
  • What is the difference between taking responsibility and taking all the blame?
  • Who stayed beside you when someone else left?

Glory-Bound and This Choice I'll Make

Told it is just a crush and they are too young, both claim the right to take their own risk: better to leap than never leave the ground.

  • When adults say "you're too young to know," what does a teen hear?
  • How can someone listen to advice and still make their own choice?
  • What makes a risk worth taking, even if it might hurt?

You Can Still Be Brave and I Can Still Be Brave

Everyone has advice about love, and it all disagrees. Fear and courage can live in the same heart.

  • Whose advice about love do you trust, and why?
  • What does being afraid and brave at the same time look like?
  • How can an adult support a teen's choice without making it for them?

Guide 4: Leave a Little Light Behind

Three songs from the season of life where memory becomes gratitude and love becomes legacy. For aging and gerontology courses, nursing, hospice and grief support, senior centers, and programs where grandparents and grandchildren listen together.

In Erikson's terms, these songs live in Stages 7 and 8: what we pass on, and making peace with the life we have lived. Stories That Remain speaks of losing a wife, so preview it before using it with people who are grieving. The album page is here.

Seventy-Five Summers

A grandfather looks backward and forward at the same time, hoping his grandchildren inherit not his memories, but the belonging that lived inside them.

  • Who first made you feel that you mattered?
  • What from your grandparents' world is still worth passing on?
  • Who could you look in the eye this week and tell they matter?

Stories That Remain

Grief teaches us that someone is gone. Stories remind us that they were here, and in many ways still are.

  • Whose story do you carry, and who still tells it with you?
  • When has someone else's memory brought a loved one back to you?
  • What kindness would you like to keep growing in others after you?

Slow-Down Sundays

The past was not perfect, but its best gifts can be passed forward: kindness, connection, and a little light for the people we love to find.

  • What did a slow Sunday look like in your family?
  • What beauty around you have you stopped noticing?
  • When will you share a meal and look someone in the eye?

A note on the Becoming album

Becoming tells one girl's story across twelve songs, for adolescents and the adults who love them. It touches on difficult experiences, including feeling unseen, body image, heartbreak, and becoming a young mother.

Counselors, health educators, and others working with teens may find it powerful. We encourage you to listen through the album before using it in a group. The album page includes a gentle note and support resources, including 988.
