The story behind the song

Before Awakenings began, there was Into The Night.

In the late 1970s and early 1980s, I commuted by train to work in San Francisco. Each day I stepped into a city where men and women slept on sidewalks, huddled in cardboard boxes, and wrapped rags around their feet to survive. The problem was impossible to ignore, yet most people seemed determined not to see it.

I wrote Into The Night because I could not look away.

Part rock anthem, part social commentary, and decades ahead of its time, the song tells the story of a homeless man slipping unnoticed into the darkness while the world passes him by. The lyrics challenge both citizens and leaders to confront a problem that belongs to all of us. In one of the song's most powerful moments, the focus shifts from the individual to the nation itself:

"America lies in the street..."

The message is simple: no society can call itself successful while its most vulnerable people are left behind.

The recording also captured something special in the chemistry of our band, The Country Rebels. Tony's powerful, gravel-edged voice brought the story to life. George's country influences grounded the song in authenticity. Von, our drummer, delivered a surprise rap section despite having never performed one before, and somehow it fit perfectly. I played keyboards, guitar, and bass, and helped shape the arrangement. Together we created one of the strongest songs we ever recorded.

At the time, I was convinced this song could make a difference. I mailed copies to record labels and producers, believing that if the right people heard it, the music might help bring attention to a growing crisis.

The recordings always came back unheard.

Looking back, that disappointment became an unexpected turning point.

Frustrated by the challenges of coordinating a band, I began experimenting with MIDI technology, connecting three keyboards to a Macintosh SE and creating complete arrangements on my own. What started as a practical solution became something much greater. Soon the melodies came faster than I could explain them, opening the creative season that would eventually become Awakenings.

For that reason, Into The Night occupies a unique place in my musical journey. It was the last song I recorded with The Country Rebels and the first step toward everything that followed.

There is one final irony.

More than forty years have passed since I wrote these lyrics, yet the homelessness crisis that inspired them remains with us. In many places, it has grown worse. The song's message is no less relevant today than it was when it was first recorded.

Perhaps that's why it has finally returned.

Not as a relic from the past, but as a reminder that some stories remain unfinished until we decide to listen.

The Country Rebels

This song is a tribute to The Country Rebels: Tony, George, Von and me. I loved playing with these guys because there were no boundaries.

Tony was the lead singer and had a beautiful gravelly voice, full of grit and power. Tony was country rock and classic rock. He was a songwriter and a talented musician. If Tony had been discovered in this very restrictive world of music, he would be signing autographs today. He was that good.

George, our rhythm guitarist, was pure classic country: Hank Williams, George Strait, and Marty Robbins.

Von was our drummer. He could play anything and make it sound good.

I played guitar, bass and keyboards, and wrote songs, and we all backed up each other's lead vocals.

We had a great time. I remember playing at Eagles Clubs, independent living facilities, bars, and pretty much any place that would have us. I remember looking forward to breakfast between 2 and 3 in the morning at Denny's. Grand Slams all around.

I remember the day we recorded this song. I didn't know at the time it would be the last time we would ever get together. There was a war starting in the Middle East, and I was called up as an Army reservist.

Style: Country Rock, Classic Rock
Feel: Compassionate, Urgent, Reflective, Defiant
