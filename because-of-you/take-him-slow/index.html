Take Him Slow

For my wife, Eileen.

The story behind the song

"Take Him Slow" is a song for my wife and partner, Eileen. If someone you love has carried you, tell them. Share your story in the comments.

The song was born in the shower, when a phrase kept circling in my head: You don't know what you don't know. It took about two weeks of showers to get a working version.

As I've grown older, I've faced significant health challenges. They forced me to confront a hard fear: what would happen to Eileen after I was gone?

That worry pulled me back to how utterly certain I was when we first married. I was so sure of everything. Only later did I realize how much I'd gotten wrong.

This song is a tribute to a woman who saw through the false bravado of a cocky young man and, with patience and gentleness, taught him to become better. She showed me how to accept aging, not with resignation, but with grace and dignity. She simply loved me into wisdom.

What the song is saying

"A man don't know what a man don't know": an old saying about learning. The more you know, the more you realize how little you know. I was a cocky young man who thought he knew everything. It takes a good woman to take him slow.

"She finished the growing my mother left undone": a boy is often only half raised when he leaves home, because he stops listening to his mother too soon. It took a good woman to finish the job.

Forty years in white: Eileen climbed the nursing ladder one rung at a time, from Medical Assistant to Professor of Nursing. Her students loved her and still do. Each time I'm admitted to the hospital, we meet former students of hers, and they take wonderful care of me.

"Helpless at the door": with all that evidence, why was I so afraid she couldn't take care of herself? Long before she met me, Eileen had endured hardships and prevailed.

The lightbulb: I thought I was the one who kept the roof above us, but she was every beam.

Eileen, thank you for almost fifty years.
