Dear Brent Dude

For my cousin, Brent Puniwai.

Ever known someone brilliant who never needed a degree to prove it? Dear Brent Dude is my song for my cousin, Brent Puniwai. Share your own "Brent" in the comments.

Watch on YouTube

The story behind the song

I wrote it in honor of his birthday anniversary, September 20.

Brent and I were birds of a feather. Quirky, experimental, and early adopters of technology. The song grew out of a letter I wrote to be read at the scattering of his ashes on the Kern River.

What the song is saying

Verse 1: The framed certificate. We have all met brilliant people who never finished college. Money, family, life got in the way. In Brent's case, the professors had nothing left to teach him.

But people with degrees can look down on people without them. Brent was the most creative, most productive producer on the team, yet his supervisor treated him as slow and berated him over small slights. (And being like me, Brent took every chance to bend the rules.)

One day he walked into the design studio and hung a framed Mensa certificate on the wall behind his table. Mensa is the international society for people who score in the top two percent on an intelligence test. Its name is Latin for "table." The condescension stopped. Why argue with stupid? Show them.

Pre-Chorus: Our private language. After Bill and Ted's Excellent Adventure we began calling each other "dude." It was our word for me and you.

Chorus: The long road west became a holy night. Brent made the ordinary strange and bright.

Verse 2: He was born and raised in California, but the islands ran deep in him. He was devoted to our genealogy, and all his Hawaiian relatives admired his engineering and his Hawaiian craft. "I should have said it plainly" is my regret that I never told him so. I thought we had forever.

Bridge: Four Corners, Chaco Canyon and Sedona. The places we traveled and camped together. We asked the dark what thought could do, then boiled coffee black enough for two.

Verse 3: Our Directed Coincidence experiment. We tried to will cowboy coffee and donuts into existence. The next morning, in a souvenir store in the middle of nowhere, there was the pot by the stove, the ladle and the donuts. As we used to say, unreal.

Final Chorus and Outro: As the Kern River carried his ashes away, I imagined them ending up in the cup of someone drinking cowboy coffee. So I ask him to save one wild coincidence for me and you.

The video

The mug at the end of the video is one Brent designed to commemorate that trip. It reads "Houhuna Institute, directed coincidence research," and it ties into the last line of the song. In Hawaiian, hou means new and huna means a hidden secret.

If you knew Brent, I hope this brings back a smile. Please share your memories of him, or of someone you know like him, in the comments.

See you on the other side, cousin.

Dave Dude
