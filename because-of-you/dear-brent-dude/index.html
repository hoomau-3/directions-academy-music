Because of You · Song 1 of 2

Dear Brent Dude

Who helped make you who you are?

Ever known someone whose curiosity made your world bigger? This song is for my cousin Brent Puniwai, who never needed a degree to prove how brilliant he was. We all have a Brent: someone whose questions, ideas, and friendship changed us. Who was yours?

The story behind the song

I wrote this song for Brent's birthday, September 20, but its roots go much deeper. Brent and I were birds of a feather: curious, unconventional, forever experimenting with new ideas and new technology long before everyone else caught up.

We could lose hours in conversation, chasing questions down unexpected paths just to see where they led.

After he died, I wrote a letter that was read when his ashes were scattered on the Kern River. This song grew from that letter. It is part remembrance, part gratitude, and part conversation with someone whose influence never really ended.

Some people leave behind possessions. Others leave behind ways of seeing the world.

Brent left me both.

What the song is saying

Verse 1: The Framed Certificate

The song opens with a true story.

We have all met brilliant people who never finished college. Money, family, circumstance, or simply a different path can interrupt a formal education. In Brent's case, I sometimes joked that the professors had run out of things to teach him.

Still, people with degrees can look down on people without them. Brent was the most creative and productive producer on his team, yet his supervisor often treated him as if he were slow and regularly berated him over small mistakes.

To be fair, Brent was also the kind of person who delighted in finding the edge of every rule and occasionally stepping over it.

One day, he walked into the design studio carrying a framed Mensa certificate and hung it on the wall behind his workstation.

Mensa is the international society for people who score in the top two percent on an intelligence test. Its name comes from the Latin word for "table."

The condescension stopped.

Why argue with people who underestimate you? Sometimes the simplest answer is to show them.

Pre-Chorus: Our Private Language

After Bill and Ted's Excellent Adventure, Brent and I started calling each other "dude."

It became our word.

A small piece of private language that meant, "I know you. You know me."

Years later, one word could still carry an entire friendship.

Chorus: The Long Road West

The chorus remembers what it felt like to travel with Brent.

He had a gift for making ordinary things feel strange, interesting, and alive. A drive became an adventure. A question became an experiment. A random stop became a story.

With Brent, the world always seemed larger than it had five minutes before.

Verse 2: The Islands Ran Deep

Although Brent was born and raised in California, Hawaii lived deep within him.

He was devoted to genealogy and family history. He cared about where we came from and about the people whose lives made ours possible. His Hawaiian relatives admired both his engineering mind and the craftsmanship he brought to traditional Hawaiian arts.

The line "I should have said it plainly" comes from regret.

I never told him enough.

I assumed there would always be another conversation, another trip, another chance to say what he had meant to me.

Like many people, I thought we had forever.

Bridge: Four Corners, Chaco Canyon, and Sedona

The bridge recalls places we explored together: Four Corners, Chaco Canyon, and Sedona.

We spent long nights around campfires asking impossible questions and following ideas wherever they led.

The line "We asked the dark what thought could do" captures those conversations.

And yes, we drank enough cowboy coffee to fuel most of them.

Verse 3: Directed Coincidence

One of our favorite experiments was something we called Directed Coincidence.

The idea was simple: could you intentionally attract an unlikely event into your life?

On one trip, we decided we wanted cowboy coffee and fresh donuts to appear somewhere out in the middle of nowhere.

The next morning we wandered into a tiny souvenir store and found exactly that: a pot by the stove, a ladle, and donuts waiting nearby.

As Brent and I would have said:

Unreal.

Final Chorus and Outro

When Brent's ashes were scattered in the Kern River, I found myself imagining where that water might eventually travel.

In my mind, some tiny part of him would end up somewhere unexpected, carried downstream into another story, another campfire, another cup of cowboy coffee.

The final lines imagine Brent still doing what he always did: turning ordinary reality into something a little stranger, a little brighter, and a little more connected than it first appears.

So I ask him for one last favor.

Save one wild coincidence for me and you.

The video

The mug at the end of the video was one Brent designed to commemorate one of our road trips. It reads:

"Houhuna Institute, Directed Coincidence Research."

It is a private joke, a souvenir from years spent chasing ideas, experiments, and improbable connections. In Hawaiian, hou means new and huna means hidden secret, which felt like the perfect name for the kind of mysteries we were always trying to explore.

The mug also connects to the final lines of the song. If you listen closely, you will hear that our old Directed Coincidence experiment never really ended. It simply became part of the story.

If you knew Brent, I hope this song brings back a smile.

Please share your memories of him, or of someone like him, in the comments. I'd love to hear the stories.

Until we meet again, cousin.

Dave Dude
